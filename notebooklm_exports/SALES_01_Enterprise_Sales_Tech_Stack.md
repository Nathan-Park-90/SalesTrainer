# Sales Engineering — Module 1: Enterprise Sales Tech Stack

## Salesforce is your Ledger

Here's a hard truth that separates elite SEs from order-takers: **you don't own the deal — your CRM does.** Everything that happens verbally in a room, every commitment made over lunch, every technical concern raised on a whiteboard — if it's not in Salesforce, it didn't happen. Not because your memory is bad, but because deals don't close in a single moment. They close in a series of coordinated efforts across weeks and months, involving five people from your company and six from the prospect's side.

**Salesforce as a System of Record:**

The Opportunity record is the single source of truth for a deal's status. Account hierarchy tells you who owns the relationship at the enterprise level. Contacts with roles (Economic Buyer, Technical Buyer, Champion) map the decision landscape. Activity timeline (calls logged, emails sent, meetings recorded) tells the story of the deal's momentum. A CRM is not bureaucracy — it's institutional knowledge that survives personnel changes.

**What Elite SEs Log (That Average SEs Don't):**

Technical objections raised and how they were resolved. Evaluation criteria the prospect stated (even casually). Competitor mentions and what the prospect said about them. Technical proof-of-concept outcomes — what worked, what didn't. Architecture decisions made during the sales cycle that will define the deployment scope.

**Opportunity Stages as a Forcing Function:**

Every stage in Salesforce represents a specific customer commitment — not just your internal confidence level. "Technical Validation" means the customer has agreed to a POC, not that you think the architecture is technically sound. Accurate stage management is what gives your manager and your company's revenue forecasting integrity.

**⚓ Anchor:** Salesforce is your trading blotter. Every trade (customer interaction, commitment, technical discovery) gets logged with timestamp, details, and outcome. A trader who doesn't log their positions doesn't know their P&L. An SE who doesn't log their deal activity doesn't know their pipeline health — and neither does their manager. The deal is the position. Salesforce is the ledger. Period.

**Pro Tip:** The most powerful Salesforce habit: log your meeting notes within 30 minutes of the conversation while details are vivid. Write them as if a colleague who wasn't in the room needs to take over the deal tomorrow. Because someday, they will.

## Gong: The Game Tape

Elite athletes watch film. Elite quarterbacks study their own throws — the good ones and the catastrophic ones. Elite traders review their decision journal every week. And elite SEs listen to their own sales calls with the same brutal, objective honesty. **Gong** is the tool that makes this possible at scale — and most SEs are terrified of it for exactly that reason.

**What Gong Does:** Records and transcribes every sales call. Uses AI to analyze talk-to-listen ratios, identify monologue moments, flag competitor mentions, track deal-advancing questions, and identify patterns in the calls of top performers vs. average performers. It turns soft skills into measurable data.

**The Talk-to-Listen Ratio:** The data consistently shows that top-performing SEs talk less. Not because silence is golden, but because questions drive deals. A 60/40 prospect-to-SE talk ratio is a healthy discovery call. A 70/30 SE-to-prospect ratio is a product pitch masquerading as a discovery call. Gong exposes the difference mercilessly.

**Mutual Action Plans in Gong:** When a prospect says "we need to evaluate this for security compliance" on a call, Gong surfaces it. The SE's job is to convert that verbal statement into a specific next step with a date and an owner in the CRM. Gong's AI tracks whether verbal commitments turn into logged actions — or evaporate.

**Using Gong to Self-Coach:** Listen specifically for: moments you talked over a prospect, monologues longer than 2 minutes, technical jargon that generated silence instead of engagement, and the exact words the prospect used to describe their pain (not your words — theirs). The prospect's language is the language of the proposal.

**⚓ Anchor:** Gong is the game tape. Every professional sports team watches game tape not to celebrate the wins but to dissect the losses — the exact moment the defense broke down, the exact play that cost the game. An SE watching their own Gong call with headphones on, grading their own performance, is doing the same thing. It's uncomfortable. It's the most efficient improvement mechanism available. Champions use it weekly. Average performers avoid it.

**Pro Tip:** Listen to calls from your top-performing colleague on Gong. Don't just copy their style — identify the specific questions they ask that generate the most valuable customer responses. Those questions are assets. Adopt and personalize them.
