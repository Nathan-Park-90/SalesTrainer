# Professional Scrum Master — Module 4: Artifacts & Commitments

## Product Backlog & Product Goal

Each Scrum artifact contains a commitment. For the Product Backlog, the commitment is the **Product Goal**.

The Product Backlog is an emergent, ordered list of what is needed to improve the product. It is the single source of work undertaken by the Scrum Team. The Product Goal describes a future state of the product which can serve as a target for the Scrum Team to plan against. The Product Goal is in the Product Backlog. The rest of the Product Backlog emerges to define "what" will fulfill the Product Goal.

**Exam Tip (PSM 1):** A Scrum Team must fulfill (or abandon) one Product Goal before taking on the next. They cannot have multiple active Product Goals.

---

## Sprint Backlog & Sprint Goal

For the Sprint Backlog, the commitment is the **Sprint Goal**.

The Sprint Backlog is composed of the Sprint Goal (why), the set of Product Backlog items selected for the Sprint (what), and an actionable plan for delivering the Increment (how). The Sprint Backlog is a plan by and for the Developers. It is highly visible, real-time picture of the work that the Developers plan to accomplish during the Sprint.

---

## The Increment & Definition of Done

For the Increment, the commitment is the **Definition of Done (DoD)**.

An Increment is a concrete stepping stone toward the Product Goal. Each Increment is additive to all prior Increments and thoroughly verified, ensuring that all Increments work together. The DoD is a formal description of the state of the Increment when it meets the quality measures required for the product.

The moment a Product Backlog item meets the Definition of Done, an Increment is born.

**Exam Tip (PSM 1):** If an item does not meet the Definition of Done, it cannot be released and cannot even be presented at the Sprint Review. It returns to the Product Backlog for future consideration. You cannot show "99% done" work in the Sprint Review.
