# Module 17: The Art of the Demo

## Discovery First, Always

**The hardest discipline in sales is shutting up when you know the answer.** You've seen this exact architecture problem 40 times. You know exactly which product solves it. The prospect is describing their pain and you want to interrupt and say "yes, we do exactly that." Don't. Not yet.



Discovery is the systematic process of understanding the customer's world before you start describing your solution. Great discovery doesn't just uncover what the problem is — it uncovers what the problem costs, who owns it, why it hasn't been solved already, and what a successful outcome looks like to different people in the room.



**The Four Discovery Dimensions:**

**Situational:** "Walk me through your current architecture. What does data flow look like today—" Understand the current state before proposing the future state.

**Problem:** "Where does that architecture break down? What does that cost you when it breaks—" Quantify pain. Abstract pain doesn't get budget approved. Quantified pain does.

**Implication:** "If this problem continues for another year at the same rate, what's the business impact—" Expand the problem from a technical annoyance to a business risk.

**Outcome:** "What does success look like 12 months from now, in terms a board presentation would use—" Understand how they'll measure whether your solution worked.



**The Questions You're Not Asking (But Should Be):**

"What have you already tried to solve this—" (Reveals failed attempts, internal politics, and where the bodies are buried.)

"Who else in your organization cares about this outcome—" (Maps the decision network.)

"What would make you confident enough to move forward—" (Surfaces unstated success criteria.)

**⚓ Anchor:** A surgeon doesn't walk into the OR without an X-ray and a full chart review, even if they're certain of the diagnosis. They confirm. They verify. They look for complications that aren't obvious from the presenting symptom. Discovery is your X-ray — the systematic evidence-gathering that ensures you're solving the actual problem, not the surface-level symptom. Skipping discovery is operating without imaging. You might get lucky. But you might also remove the wrong organ.

**Sales Tip:** The ratio that separates top-quartile SEs in Gong data: they ask more questions and talk less than average performers, but the questions they ask are more specific, more business-outcome-focused, and more likely to surface the economic buyer's priorities. Quality over quantity, always.

## Tell-Show-Tell

The average technical demo goes like this: an SE logs into a product, starts clicking around, and narrates what they're doing for 45 minutes. "And here you can see... and if I click this... and over here we have..." The prospect nods politely. They remember nothing. The deal stalls because nobody can articulate what they saw or why it mattered.



The **Tell-Show-Tell** framework transforms a demo from a feature tour into a business case story — and it's disarmingly simple:



**Tell (Before):** Before touching the product, frame what you're about to show with a business context tied directly to something the prospect said in discovery. "In your discovery call, you mentioned that your security team spends 40 hours per week manually reviewing access logs to meet your compliance requirements. I'm going to show you exactly how we reduce that to under 2 hours." Now the prospect knows what to watch for and why it matters to them specifically.



**Show:** Execute the demonstration. Keep it tight — show only what you set up in the Tell. Resist the urge to demonstrate every feature. The prospect isn't evaluating features; they're evaluating whether you solve their specific stated problem. Every click that doesn't connect to the Tell is a click that dilutes focus and burns attention budget.



**Tell (After):** After the demonstration, explicitly connect what they just saw to the business outcome. "What you just saw eliminates the 40-hour manual review process. Your compliance team gets that time back, and your audit trail is automatically generated for your SOC 2 review. Based on the $150/hour cost your team mentioned, that's roughly $300,000 annually in recovered productivity." Now the prospect has a specific memory, a specific implication, and a specific number.

**⚓ Anchor:** Tell-Show-Tell is how the best professors teach. Before the experiment: "We're testing whether X causes Y because of Z mechanism." During: the experiment runs. After: "And that's why X caused Y — which is why it matters for real-world application W." The lecture without the framing is just a chemistry demonstration. The framing without the demonstration is just a lecture. Together, they create a complete, memorable learning moment. Your demo is the experiment. Make sure they know what they're watching and why before you run it.

**Sales Tip:** The most powerful Tell is a direct quote from the prospect's own discovery call: "You said in our last call that X was costing you Y. Watch this." When they hear their own words reflected back in a solution context, the cognitive connection is instant and undeniable.
