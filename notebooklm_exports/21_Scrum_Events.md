# Scrum Events

## The Sprint

Sprints are the heartbeat of Scrum. They are fixed length events of one month or less. **A new Sprint starts immediately after the conclusion of the previous Sprint.** There are no "Phase 1" or "Integration" sprints in Scrum.


During the Sprint:
- No changes are made that would endanger the Sprint Goal.
- Quality does not decrease.
- The Product Backlog is refined as needed.
- Scope may be clarified and renegotiated with the Product Owner as more is learned.
**Exam Tip (PSM 1):** Sprints are containers for all other events. An exam trap is asking if you can extend a Sprint to finish work. The answer is NEVER. A Sprint is a strict timebox. If the work isn't done, it goes back to the Product Backlog.

---

## Sprint Planning

Sprint Planning initiates the Sprint. It is timeboxed to a maximum of **8 hours for a one-month Sprint**. The 2020 Scrum Guide defines three topics that must be addressed during this event:


**Topic 1: Why is this Sprint valuable—** The PO proposes how the product could increase its value. The whole team collaborates to define a *Sprint Goal*.
**Topic 2: What can be Done this Sprint—** The Developers select items from the Product Backlog to include in the current Sprint.
**Topic 3: How will the chosen work get done—** The Developers decompose the selected items into an actionable plan (usually 1-day tasks).
**Exam Tip (PSM 1):** Only the Developers can decide how many items they pull into a Sprint. The PO cannot force them to take more work than they believe they can complete.

---

## Daily Scrum

The purpose of the Daily Scrum is to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog as necessary. It is a **15-minute event for the Developers**. If the PO or Scrum Master are actively working on items in the Sprint Backlog, they participate as Developers.


The Scrum Master ensures the meeting happens, but the Developers are responsible for conducting it. It is not a status meeting for management; it is a planning meeting for the Developers.
**Exam Tip (PSM 1):** The Daily Scrum does not require the classic "Three Questions" (What did I do yesterday, what will I do today, what are my blockers). The 2020 Guide removed this mandate. Developers can structure it however they want, as long as it focuses on progress toward the Sprint Goal.

---

## Sprint Review & Retrospective

**Sprint Review:** Purpose is to inspect the outcome of the Sprint and determine future adaptations. The Scrum Team presents the results of their work to key stakeholders and progress toward the Product Goal is discussed. Timeboxed to 4 hours for a one-month Sprint. It is a working session, not just a presentation.


**Sprint Retrospective:** Purpose is to plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness. The Scrum Team inspects how the last Sprint went with regards to individuals, interactions, processes, tools, and their Definition of Done. Timeboxed to 3 hours for a one-month Sprint. It concludes the Sprint.
