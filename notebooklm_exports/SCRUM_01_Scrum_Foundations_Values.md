# Professional Scrum Master — Module 1: Scrum Foundations & Values

## Empiricism: The 3 Pillars

Scrum is not a methodology; it is a lightweight framework based on empiricism and lean thinking. Empiricism asserts that knowledge comes from experience and making decisions based on what is observed. The 2020 Scrum Guide defines three critical pillars that uphold every empirical process:

**1. Transparency:** The emergent process and work must be visible to those performing the work and those receiving the work. Without transparency, inspection is misleading.

**2. Inspection:** Scrum artifacts and progress toward agreed goals must be inspected frequently and diligently to detect potentially undesirable variances.

**3. Adaptation:** If any aspects of a process deviate outside acceptable limits or if the resulting product is unacceptable, the process or the material being processed must be adjusted immediately.

**⚓ Anchor:** Empiricism is navigating a ship by looking at the stars (Inspection), updating the map (Transparency), and turning the wheel (Adaptation). Traditional waterfall is drawing a line on a map before leaving port and refusing to look out the window for the entire journey.

**Exam Tip (PSM 1):** The exam will test if you understand that Inspection without Adaptation is considered pointless in Scrum. Adaptation must happen *as soon as possible* to minimize further deviation.

## The 5 Scrum Values

The successful use of Scrum depends on people becoming more proficient in living five core values. Without these, the three pillars of empiricism crumble.

**1. Commitment:** To achieving the goals of the Scrum Team and supporting each other.

**2. Focus:** On the work of the Sprint and making the best possible progress toward these goals.

**3. Openness:** About the work and the challenges.

**4. Respect:** For each other to be capable, independent people.

**5. Courage:** To do the right thing and work on tough problems.

**Exam Tip (PSM 1):** The Scrum Values give direction to the Scrum Team with regard to their work, actions, and behavior. The exam might ask what gives life to the empirical pillars: the answer is the Scrum Values.
